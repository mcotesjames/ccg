Status tabs

Segmented filter: the active tab fuses with the band below

Interaction notes
  • Click: the active plate flips white to deep green with lime text instantly; inactive tabs warm on hover, also instantly.
  • Anatomy: each tab pairs its label with a zero-padded count set smaller and lighter; tabs are divided by hairlines inside a hairline frame.

03

Text filter

Lead-size filter tabs: gold underline marks the active choice

Scroll or drag the rows — switch viewport to see the overflow

Interaction notes
  • The frame above is a real viewport running the production component inside the wrappers the news archive and portfolio browser put around it. The gold dashed rule marks the content edge, so the row's behaviour at that edge is visible rather than guessed at.
  • Overflow: a set that outgrows its column scrolls horizontally within the row — the same treatment the service tabs use. Switch to the mobile viewport and drag the six-option row sideways; the three-option row above it fits and stays put.
  • Drag: a row that overflows can be dragged with the pointer, scrubbing 1:1. Touch and trackpads can already flick a scroll container; a mouse cannot, so this exists for mouse users and is skipped entirely on rows that fit.
  • Drag versus click: movement under 5px is still a click and selects the filter. Past that the gesture becomes a drag, the cursor turns to a closed hand, text selection is suppressed, and the click that follows the release is swallowed — so dragging a row never changes the filter by accident.
  • No scrollbar: the scrollbar is hidden on purpose, so the row reads as text rather than as a control. The options that run past the edge are the affordance: never let the last visible option end flush with the column, or the row looks complete when it is not.
  • Never centre this row. The row must stay left-aligned at every width. Centring an overflowing row overflows it at both ends, pushing the first options past the left edge where scrolling cannot reach them — and a scroll container cannot rescue what sits before its own origin.
  • Below tablet width: the filter row and the year dropdown stop sitting side by side and stack, the row aligning left and the dropdown stretching to full width.
  • Sizing: at the 402 design width the content column is 354px, which shows roughly three to four short labels before the row starts scrolling. That is a display limit, not a content limit — the set can run as long as it needs to.
  • Small variant: narrow enough that the portfolio's three-option facet clears mobile without scrolling.

Category pills

Rounded outline filter on dark grounds: active fills lime

Interaction notes
  • Click: toggles the pill on or off instantly; active pills fill lime with deep green text, and any number can be active at once.

Infinite auto-scroll · drag · custom cursor · detail modal

Interaction notes

Category

Cards

Two families and the rule that separates them: a plate means the card is a door to a place; a green title sweep means content to read. Almost every card in the design is one of these two with rows added or dropped — so each section below shows the family, then its settings.

01

Plate card

Doors to a place: image over a plate, no title sweep

Rule of use

  • The plate means navigation. Use this family wherever the card is a door to a place or a section — a property, a similar property, a chapter of the site. Content you scan and read uses the editorial card instead.
  • Pick by how much the destination needs explaining: the full plate carries a description and an explicit invitation; the property plate carries a name, its district and its type, and nothing else.
  • No card in this family sweeps its title. The plate shift is the hover state, which is what separates it from the editorial family at a glance.

  • Hover / focus (both settings): the image zooms gently over 0.8s, the plate shifts ground instantly, and the arrow on the view row nudges 4px right over 0.35s. The whole card is one link.

02

Editorial card

Content you scan and read: green title sweep, image zoom

Rule of use

  • The green title sweep means editorial. Use this family for content the reader scans and reads — news, awards, stories, businesses — and for cross-page routing at the end of a section. Doors to a property or a place use the plate card.
  • One card, seven rows. Every row beneath the image is optional, so the same component runs from a bare title-and-arrow up to the fully loaded card below. Build it by dropping rows, never by restyling them or resetting their order.
  • Whatever the setting, the hover is identical: image zoom, green underline sweep, arrow nudge. That consistency is the point of keeping them one family.

Interaction notes
  • No JavaScript. This is a native <details> and <summary> pair. Open and close, keyboard operation, and the screen-reader announcement all come from the browser — there is nothing to bind and nothing to initialise. Do not reimplement it with a click handler.
  • Open / close: the panel snaps; there is no height animation. Native disclosure cannot animate its own height, and no attempt is made to fake one. Only the toggle animates.
  • The toggle: a 32px hairline square carrying a plus drawn from two 1px bars. Opening fills it deep green with a white plus, and the vertical bar rotates flat over 0.3s on the house easing curve, leaving a minus. The square is decorative and hidden from assistive tech — the summary already announces its own state.
  • Hover: the question moves from deep green to green over 0.25s. The whole summary row is the target, not just the words.
  • Several open at once: the native default is kept — opening one item never closes another. There is no single-open variant, so do not assume one.
  • Opening state: the first item ships open, so the block never presents as a wall of closed rows.
  • Anatomy: a hairline above every item and one below the last. The question runs at lead size with the toggle pushed to the right edge and centred against the row; the answer is grey, indented 24px and capped at 780px. On mobile that indent goes and the answer sits flush.
  • Closing row: optional, and not part of the accordion — a note left, a secondary link CTA right, wrapping when the pair will not fit. Both current uses route the reader on rather than leaving them at a dead end.
  • Keyboard: the summary is focusable by default and toggles on Enter or Space. The default marker is suppressed for WebKit, which is what lets the custom toggle stand in for it.
  • Where the code lives: components.css, under ACCORDION. The pages supply only the section band around it — its padding and its ground, which is cream on the contact page and white on the internship page.

Interaction notes
  • Auto-advance: the index steps to the next service every 7 seconds and wraps at the end. A gold hairline fills left to right under the active row over exactly that interval, so the reader can see how long is left.
  • Pause: the whole stage pauses on hover and on keyboard focus, and while the browser tab is in the background. The gold line freezes where it is. On leaving, the line restarts from zero and the timer resets — a reader is never rushed off a panel they just arrived at.
  • Click a row: switches immediately and restarts the timer.
  • Keyboard: the index is a real tablist — one tab stop reaches it, then the arrow keys move between services. Up and left go back, down and right go forward, both wrapping at the ends; focus follows the selection.
  • Panel change: the outgoing panel is replaced by the incoming one, which fades and rises into place over 0.5s on the house easing curve.
  • Index anatomy: hairline-ruled rows in a fixed 504px column. Each row pairs a gold serif-italic numeral in a 48px column with its label; resting labels are muted white, hover lifts them to full white, and the active row is lime.
  • Panel anatomy: a 16:9 card split in half — image left, green copy plate right — so image and plate read as one card. The fixed ratio is what stops the stage jumping as services change. The plate holds a title, body copy and an optional aside: an inclusions list with lime dot markers, and a light secondary CTA.
  • Grid maths at 1512: a 1384 content column splits 504 + 64 + 816, and the 816 panel at 16:9 lands on an integer 459 high.
  • Responsive: below 1000 the stage stacks, the panel drops its fixed ratio and the media becomes 2:1; at mobile the media is 3:2, the plate insets tighten and the index steps down one size. The numbered index stays a vertical list at every width — it never becomes a horizontal tab row.
  • Reduced motion: the auto-advance never starts, the gold line sits full rather than animating, and panels change without the fade. Clicks and arrow keys still work.
  • Wiring: no page JS — scripts/components.js binds every .service-tabs on load. Panels are matched to rows by aria-controls and id; mark the opening pair .is-active, and set the interval with data-autoplay in milliseconds. Dropping that attribute leaves a manual tablist with no timer and no gold line.
  • Ground: the module sets its own colours but not its background — the wrapping section supplies the deep green.

  • Responsive: below 1000 the stage flips to a single column with the index above the detail and both aligned left. On mobile the title drops to 40/44 and the index rows to 16px.
  • Reduced motion: the rotation never starts and the module rests on its first story. Clicking the index still works.
  • Two gotchas for the build: the component ships display: none and does nothing until a page turns it on, and it ships empty — media, index and detail are all written by page JS from a story array. There is no markup contract for a story, so the array is the contract.
  • Keyboard gap: index rows are buttons, so they focus and activate, but there are no arrow keys and the band does not pause on focus — only on hover. A keyboard user tabbing the index has stories changing under them. The service tabs handle this correctly and are the pattern to copy.

Sticky enquiry panel

Routing panel that holds beside a long form, then releases

Interaction notes
  • The frame above is a real viewport. The form column is a plain placeholder block — the panel beside it is the production component. Scroll inside the frame to watch it hold, then release.
  • Sticking: the panel scrolls with the page until it reaches 32px from the top of the viewport, then holds there while the form column keeps moving past it. There is no JavaScript and no scroll listener — it is position: sticky and nothing else.
  • Releasing: the panel is only ever sticky within its own grid cell. When the bottom of that cell reaches the panel, the panel is carried away with it — so it never escapes the enquiry section and never overlaps what follows.
  • The rule that makes it work: the layout grid sets align-items: start. Without it, grid items stretch to the full height of the row, the panel's cell becomes exactly as tall as the panel, and there is no travel for it to stick inside — the rule looks applied but nothing ever sticks. This is the single most common way a sticky sidebar silently fails, and it is why the property belongs to the layout as much as to the panel.
  • Layout: a fluid form column and a fixed 448px panel with a 64px gap — 872 + 64 + 448 = 1384 at the design width.
  • Anatomy: a solid green plate in inverse ink: title, a short blurb, caption-size labels over lime contact links that turn white on hover, and a quieter closing note above a hairline.
  • Live content: the panel carries aria-live="polite", because the form's enquiry-type select rewrites its title, blurb and contacts. A screen-reader user hears the routing change without leaving the field they are in.
  • Responsive, in three steps: below 1000 the layout collapses to one column and the panel takes order: -1, moving above the form as an introduction — still sticky, but with a single-row cell it no longer has anywhere to travel. Below 680 it drops to position: static and order: 0, returning below the form as a closing footnote. The mobile button shows that last state; the middle one appears between the two widths.
  • The reordering is deliberate: on a tablet the panel tells you where your enquiry will go before you start writing; on a phone, where it would eat the first screen, it waits until you have finished.

Interaction notes
  • Anatomy: a caption-size grey label above the control, and the control itself stripped to a single hairline underneath. No border, no background, no radius — text sits directly on the page and the line does the work.

Interaction notes
  • Two shapes, on purpose: radios stay native and round; checkboxes are custom-drawn and square, because the UA radius is the one thing that cannot be styled away. Round means one of these, square means any of these — the shape is the affordance.
