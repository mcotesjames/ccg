Site navigation

Sticky reveal + mega menu: contained viewport below

Interaction notes
  • The frame above is a real viewport running the production header. Scroll inside it, and hover a nav item at the top to open the mega menu.
  • At top: the header is transparent over the hero, with white text and an inverted logo.
  • Scroll down past 92px: the header slides away upward over 0.42s on the house easing curve.
  • Scroll up: any upward scroll brings the header back as a fixed solid white bar with dark ink, the logo un-inverted and the CTA in its solid deep green form. Back at the very top it reattaches and turns transparent again.
  • Nav links: hover or focus draws a thin underline from the left over 0.3s; leaving retracts it to the right.

Mobile navigation: switch to the 414px viewport above

Interaction notes
  • The bar (mobile): drops to 72px tall and keeps three elements: logo, a slimmed Sales & Leasing plate, and the burger. Search moves into the menu panel.
  • Burger: three hairlines with the middle one shorter; hover extends it to full width. Open crosses the outer pair and retires the middle line.
  • Tap burger: a full-screen white panel slides down from the top over 0.55s; the section rows cascade in as the sheet lands, the bar fuses with it and page scrolling locks.
  • Tap a section: the five-row list sweeps left and fades as the child links cascade in from the right with a Back row and the section title. Back reverses; closing the panel resets to the top level.
  • Utility foot: a full-width Search row above Contact Us and the language toggle. The active language reads by weight, not underline.
  • Escape: closes the panel and returns focus to the burger.

Mega menu: hover a nav item in the desktop viewport

Interaction notes
  • Open (hover / focus / click a nav item): the panel fades in and drops 10px into place over roughly a third of a second. The header turns solid while open, and the open item keeps its underline.
  • Moving between items: repopulates the panel in place, with no close-and-reopen flicker.
  • Layout: four columns: overview (kicker, title, description), two numbered link lists split evenly, and a feature card with image, title and a read-the-story link. A utility row beneath carries Contact Us and the language toggle.
  • Link rows: hover or focus shifts the row slightly right, inks it green and fades in a green arrow. Numerals are set in the serif italic.
  • Feature card: the image zooms gently over 0.7s; the link underline darkens to green.
  • Close: a moment after the pointer leaves the header (brushing past never flickers it); immediately on an outside click; Escape closes and returns focus to the triggering item.
  • Below desktop width the mega menu does not exist; navigation moves to the mobile panel above.

Footer

Global footer: one source, identical on every page

Interaction notes
  • One shared footer, identical on every page; the sitemap is maintained in a single place.
  • Layout: six equal columns on the deep green ground: five navigation groups mirroring the main menu, plus a contact column with address, phone and email. The bottom row holds the inverted wordmark, centred legal links and the social icons.
  • Hover (social): icons lift 2px over 0.25s. Navigation links rest at a softened white.
  • Responsive: the navigation grid steps from six columns to three, then two on mobile; the bottom row stacks and centres: the wordmark grows to three-quarters of the column, the legal row tightens, and the footer gains extra bottom padding.
03

Hero parallax

Scroll-linked exit: the hero lags at half speed and darkens as it goes

Interaction notes
  • The frame above is a real viewport. The lockup and video are placeholder copy and the poster frame — the scroll behaviour is the component. Scroll inside the frame to watch the hero fall behind and darken; scroll back slowly to watch it re-emerge.
  • The lag: on every frame the hero is translated down by half the scroll — translateY(scrollY × 0.5) — so it gives up only half of each scrolled pixel and the content after it slides up and over it rather than pushing it away. The hero sits at z-index: 0 with will-change: transform; a sibling rule (.hero ~ section, .hero ~ div) lifts everything after it to z-index: 1 so the overlap resolves the right way.
  • The darkening: a .hero-scrim layer — a gradient from solid #05191d at the foot to 75% at the top — fades from 0 towards an 0.8 ceiling. Progress is scrollY / heroHeight clamped 0–1, so the scrim lands at 0.8 exactly as the hero's foot clears the viewport. It sits at z-index: 3, above the hero's own static scrims.
  • Two kinds of scrim: the hero's ::after gradients (top for nav legibility, foot for the lockup) are static and always present; the darkening scrim is a separate layer that only exists while the effect is bound. Don't merge them.
  • Injection: the scrim is created and appended by the script, never authored in markup — the hero's markup contract stays clean and the effect is removable by deleting the script alone.
  • Listener: one passive scroll listener throttled through requestAnimationFrame — the handler sets a flag, the frame callback clears it, and layout reads (offsetHeight, scrollY) happen only inside the frame.
  • Reduced motion: the listener never binds — the hero scrolls away normally and the darkening scrim stays at opacity 0. This is checked once at load, not per frame.
  • Source: index.html, verbatim — the production hero runs a looping video behind the same layers. Currently the home page only; a second parallax hero is the trigger to promote the script into scripts/components.js beside ccgParallax().
