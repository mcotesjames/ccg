Site navigation

Sticky reveal + mega menu: contained viewport below

Interaction notes
  • The frame above is a real viewport running the production header. Scroll inside it, and hover a nav item at the top to open the mega menu.
  • At top: the header is transparent over the hero, with white text and an inverted logo.
  • Scroll down: once the reader is a short way past the top, the header slides away upward over 0.42s on the house easing curve.
  • Scroll up: any upward scroll brings the header back as a fixed solid white bar with dark ink, the logo un-inverted and the CTA in its solid deep green form. Back at the very top it reattaches and turns transparent again.
  • Nav links: hover or focus draws a thin underline from the left over 0.3s; leaving retracts it to the right.

Mobile navigation: switch to the 414px viewport above

Interaction notes
  • The bar (mobile): drops to a slimmer bar keeping three elements: logo, a slimmed Sales & Leasing plate, and the burger. Search moves into the menu panel.
  • Burger: three hairlines with the middle one shorter; hover extends it to full width. Open crosses the outer pair and retires the middle line.
  • Tap burger: a full-screen white panel slides down from the top over 0.55s; the section rows cascade in as the sheet lands, the bar fuses with it and page scrolling locks.
  • While open: the bar is always on screen, pinned to the top of the viewport, whatever state it was in (parked off-canvas or scrolled away). It returns to that state when the panel closes.
  • Tap a section: a natural swipe, no fades: the five-row list and the section pane (Back row, section title, child links) sit side by side and travel a full pane width left together over 0.5s. The section title is ruled off in ground yellow. Below the child links sits the section's sample story, the same one its mega menu features: caption label, 16:9 image, title ruled off by a hairline, and a plain Read the story link, whose arrow nudges when the story is hovered; the pane scrolls when the story runs past the foot. Back swipes them right again; closing the panel resets to the top level.
  • Hover (rows and links): the secondary CTA's underline sweep: a green line fills each row's hairline left to right over 0.45s, and the row's arrow nudges 4px forward. Section rows answer hover straight away; their opening cascade delay only applies to the entrance. Every link in the menu (section rows, child links, Search, Back, Contact Us) rests grey and turns black on hover. The panes' stage runs to the screen edges, so nudged arrows are never clipped and the swipe travels edge to edge.
  • Utility foot: a Search row, styled exactly like the section rows above (same scale, arrow and underline sweep) but held at the foot, above Contact Us and the language toggle. The active language reads by weight, not underline.
  • Tap Search: the menu's own search, not the desktop panel. A search pane swipes in like a section (same 0.5s swipe, Back row) with the field focused so the keyboard rises. Typing two or more characters lists the top three matches and a "See all" link to the results page; enter goes to the results page too. The field takes the section-title scale and the rows' underline sweep on focus. Back or closing the menu clears it.
  • Escape: closes the panel and returns focus to the burger.

Mega menu: hover a nav item in the desktop viewport

Interaction notes
  • Open (hover / focus / click a nav item): the panel fades in and drops slightly into place over roughly a third of a second. The header turns solid while open, and the open item keeps its underline.
  • Moving between items: repopulates the panel in place, with no close-and-reopen flicker.
  • Layout: three columns: overview (kicker, title, description, all in black), a single link list, and a feature card with a 16:9 image, a title ruled off by a hairline, and a plain Read the story link whose arrow nudges when the card is hovered. The three columns sit an equal distance apart, with nothing but the gap between them. The kicker sits a small step above the title. A utility row beneath carries Contact Us and the language toggle.
  • Link rows: plain black labels at body size, no numerals, rows divided by hairlines. The row never moves: on hover or focus a green line grows along its hairline left to right over 0.45s (the secondary CTA sweep, as in the mobile menu); no arrow.
  • Feature card: the image zooms gently over 0.7s; the link underline darkens to green.
  • Close: a moment after the pointer leaves the header (brushing past never flickers it); immediately on an outside click; Escape closes and returns focus to the triggering item.
  • Below desktop width the mega menu does not exist; navigation moves to the mobile panel above.
02

Footer

Global footer: one source, identical on every page

Interaction notes
  • One shared footer, identical on every page; the sitemap is maintained in a single place.
  • Layout: six equal columns on the deep green ground: five navigation groups mirroring the main menu, plus a contact column with address, phone and email. The bottom row holds the inverted wordmark, centred legal links and the social icons.
  • Hover (links): navigation and legal links rest at a softened white and brighten to full white over 0.25s.
  • Hover (social): icons turn from white to lime over 0.25s, without moving.
  • Responsive: the navigation grid steps from six columns to three, then two on mobile; the bottom row stacks and centres: the wordmark grows to three-quarters of the column, the legal row tightens, and the footer gains extra bottom padding.
03

Hero parallax

Scroll-linked exit: the hero lags at half speed and darkens as it goes

Interaction notes
  • The frame above is a real viewport. The lockup and video are placeholder copy and the poster frame, the scroll behaviour is the component. Scroll inside the frame to watch the hero fall behind and darken; scroll back slowly to watch it re-emerge.
  • The lag: the hero gives up only half of each scrolled pixel, so the content after it slides up and over it rather than pushing it away, everything that follows the hero rides above its lagging exit.
  • The darkening: a separate scrim, deepest at the foot, easing off towards the top, builds from clear towards a strong ceiling as the reader scrolls, never fully black, and reaches full strength exactly as the hero's foot clears the viewport. It sits above the hero's own static scrims.
  • Two kinds of scrim: the hero's own gradients (top for nav legibility, foot for the lockup) are static and always present; the darkening scrim is a separate layer that only exists while the effect runs. Don't merge them.
  • Injection: the scrim is created and appended by the script, never authored in markup, the hero's markup contract stays clean and the effect is removable by deleting the script alone.
  • Performance: the effect tracks scrolling at the display's frame rate and never more often, so the hero moves smoothly without taxing the page.
  • Reduced motion: the listener never binds, the hero scrolls away normally and the darkening scrim stays at opacity 0. This is checked once at load, not per frame.
  • Source: the home page, verbatim, the production hero runs a looping video behind the same layers. Currently the home page only; a second parallax hero is the trigger to promote the effect into the shared script.
