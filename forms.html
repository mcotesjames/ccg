Forms

Every input style and behaviour on the site, from the contact form to the site search, plus the panel that routes an enquiry beside it. The house treatment is the underline: no boxes, no fills, no rounded corners, a control is a caption label over a line, and the line darkens as you approach it.

01

Text fields

Caption label over an underline · single line, multi-line, hint

Settings

A caption-size line under the control, for guidance rather than errors.

Interaction notes
  • Anatomy: a caption-size grey label above the control, and the control itself stripped to a single hairline underneath. No border, no background, no radius, text sits directly on the page and the line does the work.
  • Hover and focus: the underline darkens from hairline grey to deep green over 0.25s. Focus and hover look identical, and the browser's own focus ring is suppressed, the line is the focus indicator.
  • Placeholder: set in sage, a step lighter than entered text, so a filled field and an empty one are told apart by colour as well as content.
  • Multi-line: the same control with a taller floor. It resizes vertically only, horizontal resize would break the column.
  • Hint: caption size in grey, under the control. This is guidance, not validation, there is no error style in the system yet.
  • Layout: fields stack in a single column at an even rhythm. Pairs that belong together share a two-up row, which collapses to one column on mobile.
  • Semantics carry the weight: each field declares its real input type and autofill purpose, that is what gives the right mobile keyboard and lets a browser fill the form. They are part of the spec, not optional extras.

Choosing an option can drive the rest of the page, not just the field.

Interaction notes
  • Resting: reads exactly like a text field, caption label, current value, hairline underline, with a chevron pushed to the right edge.
  • Open: the chevron flips from down to up over 0.25s and a white panel drops from the underline, fading and sliding into place over 0.2s. The panel spans the full width of the field, which is what separates this from the archive dropdown, where the panel is only as wide as its content.
  • Options: resting grey; hover or focus fills cream and inks deep green; the current choice keeps deep green ink and a thin gold bar down its left edge. Past five or six options the panel caps its height and scrolls.
  • Choose: writes the label into the trigger, moves the gold bar, closes the panel and returns focus to the trigger.
  • Close: a click outside, or Escape, which also returns focus to the trigger.
  • Beyond the field: on the contact page the choice also rewrites the routing panel beside the form, so the reader sees the right team and address before they finish writing. Treat the select as a page control, not just an input.
  • Keyboard gap: the trigger and Escape work, but the panel has no arrow-key navigation, no Home or End, and no type-ahead, the options announce themselves as a listbox, so the roles promise a keyboard the panel does not yet deliver. The same gap exists on the archive dropdown.
03

Choice controls

Round native radios, square custom checkboxes

Settings

A question answered by a radio pair
Interaction notes
  • Two shapes, on purpose: radios stay native and round; checkboxes are custom-drawn and square, because the UA radius is the one thing that cannot be styled away. Round means one of these, square means any of these, the shape is the affordance.
  • Checkbox: a small white square with a hairline border. Hover darkens the border to deep green; checked fills solid green with a drawn white tick rather than an image.
  • Radio: the browser's native control, tinted to the house green. Nothing else is restyled.
  • Focus: checkboxes take a green outline ring sitting just off the control, the one place in the system that keeps a visible focus ring rather than replacing it with an underline. Do not remove it.
  • Hit area: every control lives inside its own label, so the entire row including the wrapped text is clickable. Never attach the label as a separate neighbour.
  • Spacing: checkbox rows take a wider gap than the radio pair, since their labels run to full sentences while a radio's is a single word.
  • Links inside labels: green with a faint underline that solidifies on hover. Clicking the link still toggles the control, a known rough edge on the legal rows, where the reader may mean to open the policy.
  • Long labels: where the wording runs to several lines the control aligns to the first line rather than the middle of the block.

Interaction notes
  • The plate: declarations sit on a cream plate inside the white form, so the legal block reads as a distinct region rather than more form. Rows run at a tight, even rhythm inside it.
  • The gating row: question left, answers right, on one baseline. On mobile it stacks, question over answers.
  • Unfold: answering Yes opens the conditional panel, it grows from zero to its natural height over 0.5s on the house easing curve, its inner padding easing in with it. Answering No folds it away again. This is a grid-row animation, not a height one, so the panel never needs a measured height and copy of any length works.
  • Closed state: a folded panel leaves no phantom space between the question and the rows beneath, collapsing removes its footprint entirely, not just its visibility.
  • The panel: white against the cream plate with a thin gold bar down its left edge, marking it as conditional. Inside: an intro, a red required-fields warning, green group headings, and consent rows.
  • Reduced motion: the panel opens and closes with no transition.
  • Submit: the solid primary CTA as a true submit button, visually identical to its link form once the browser's default button chrome is neutralised, which is the only difference between the two.
  • Not built yet: browser validation is switched off and there is no client-side validation, no error state, no success state and no submit behaviour. The required-fields warning is static copy that shows whenever the panel is open, not a response to anything. Error and success styling has to be designed before this form can ship.
05

Search field

Large underlined query field · submit glyph inside the line

Interaction notes
  • The query field at the top of the search results page, where it arrives pre-filled with whatever the reader searched for in the header.
  • Anatomy: the house form treatment at a much larger scale: a grey caption label above, then the query set large in deep green on a single hairline, with a magnifier glyph sitting inside the line at its right end. No box, no fill.
  • Hover and focus: the hairline darkens to deep green over 0.25s, as on every other field; the browser's focus ring is replaced by the line. The magnifier turns from ink to green on hover or focus.
  • Placeholder: set in sage, a step lighter than entered text.
  • Submit: pressing enter or the magnifier runs the search and refreshes the results beneath without leaving the page. The text never runs under the magnifier.
  • Responsive: on mobile the query steps down in size, and a long placeholder trails off with an ellipsis rather than breaking mid-word.
06

Sticky enquiry panel

Routing panel that holds beside a long form, then releases

Interaction notes
  • The frame above is a real viewport. The form column is a plain placeholder block, the panel beside it is the production component. Scroll inside the frame to watch it hold, then release.
  • Sticking: the panel scrolls with the page until it nears the top of the viewport, then holds there while the form column keeps moving past it. Nothing is scripted, the browser's native sticky positioning does all of it.
  • Releasing: the panel is only ever sticky within its own grid cell. When the bottom of that cell reaches the panel, the panel is carried away with it, so it never escapes the enquiry section and never overlaps what follows.
  • The rule that makes it work: the panel is only sticky within its own layout cell, so the layout must let that cell run taller than the panel. When the layout stretches the panel to fill its row instead, there is no travel left to stick inside, everything looks applied but nothing ever sticks. This is the single most common way a sticky sidebar silently fails, and it is why the behaviour belongs to the layout as much as to the panel.
  • Layout: a fluid form column beside a fixed-width panel, roughly a two-to-one split with a generous gutter between them.
  • Anatomy: a solid green plate in inverse ink: title, a short blurb, caption-size labels over lime contact links that turn white on hover, and a quieter closing note above a hairline.
  • Live content: the panel is a polite live region, because the form's enquiry-type select rewrites its title, blurb and contacts. A screen-reader user hears the routing change without leaving the field they are in.
  • Responsive, in three steps: at tablet width the layout collapses to one column and the panel moves above the form as an introduction, still technically sticky, but with nowhere left to travel. At phone width it stops sticking altogether and returns below the form as a closing footnote. The mobile button shows that last state; the middle one appears between the two widths.
  • The reordering is deliberate: on a tablet the panel tells you where your enquiry will go before you start writing; on a phone, where it would eat the first screen, it waits until you have finished.
